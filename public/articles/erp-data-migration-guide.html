ERP data migration: what to clean up before you move

Implementation · Published November 12, 2026 · By the Nastaliq team

The short answer

Migrate the records you need to run the business tomorrow: items, customers, suppliers, open orders, open bills, stock and opening balances. Clean them first, load them in a trial run and check the totals against your accountant.

Data migration decides whether the new system starts trusted or starts doubted. It is worth doing carefully, and it is easier when you prepare before anyone loads a record.

Decide what comes across

Move what the business needs to run tomorrow: items, customers, suppliers, open orders, open bills, stock on hand by location and opening balances. Old closed transactions can stay in the old system as a reference.

Clean the item list

Remove duplicates, retire items you no longer sell, and make units consistent. An item sold by the case and counted by the unit needs both defined. Give every item one code and one description.

Clean customers and suppliers

Merge duplicates, fix addresses and tax details, and mark inactive accounts. Customer-specific prices need to come across with the customer they belong to.

Count your stock

Do a physical count close to the cut-over date. Loading yesterday's guess as opening stock means starting with the error you were trying to fix.

Agree opening balances with your accountant

Receivables, payables, bank and stock value should match the books on the cut-over date. Have your accountant confirm them.

Run a trial load

Load a copy first. Check a sample of items, customers and balances by hand, fix what is wrong, then load for real.

Verify the totals

After the final load, compare stock value, customer balances and supplier balances with the old system. If the totals agree, the migration worked.

Plan for it as separate work

Migration is its own task, with its own time and cost, apart from configuring the system. Ask for it as a separate line in any quote. At Nastaliq it is a paid add-on, quoted after we have seen what you use today.

Common questions

How much history should we migrate?

Usually opening balances and open items, not every transaction since the company began. Keep the old records available for reference and bring across only what the new system needs to run.

Why is data migration quoted separately?

Because the work depends on the state of your data. A clean list loads in an afternoon. A spreadsheet with duplicates and mixed units takes much longer.

How do we know the migration worked?

Compare totals: stock value, customer balances and supplier balances in the new system against the old one, and have your accountant sign off the opening balances.
