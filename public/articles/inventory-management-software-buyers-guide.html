What to look for in inventory management software

Inventory · Published November 12, 2026 · By the Nastaliq team

The short answer

Good inventory software records every receipt, transfer and sale as it happens, scans barcodes, tracks stock by location and feeds the accounts. If it does not connect to purchasing and accounting, you are still re-typing.

Inventory software is easy to buy and easy to get wrong. The right choice depends on how stock moves through your business. These are the features that decide it.

Barcode scanning

Typing item codes is where errors begin. Look for scanning at receiving, transfer, counting and sale, ideally from a phone so nobody has to buy a dedicated scanner.

Stock by location

If you have more than one room, warehouse, truck or store, the system must know which units are where, and record every transfer between them.

Reorder levels and alerts

You should hear about low stock before it runs out. Check that the system can set a minimum per item and tell you when stock falls below it.

A link to purchasing

Receiving against a purchase order, not from memory, keeps the record honest. Look for purchase orders, receipts and supplier bills in one flow.

A link to sales

A sale should reduce stock the moment it is entered. Otherwise the shelf and the system disagree by the end of the day.

A link to accounting

Stock has a value, and the value belongs in the books. When receipts and sales post to the accounts automatically, month-end gets shorter.

Cycle counts

Counting a few fast-moving items every week beats one big count a year. Check that the system supports counts and shows the difference between the count and the record.

When to choose an ERP instead

If you are connecting inventory software to a separate accounting package and a separate order system, you are already running a homemade ERP. One system removes the links you have to maintain.

Common questions

Do we need an ERP, or is stand-alone inventory software enough?

If stock only has to be counted, stand-alone software can do. Once purchasing, sales and accounting depend on the stock figure, one system avoids the re-typing and the drift between tools.

Is barcode scanning worth it?

Yes. Scanning at receiving, transfer and sale removes most typing errors, and a phone camera is enough to start.

How do we handle more than one warehouse?

Choose a system that keeps stock by location and records transfers between locations, so every unit is somewhere.
