Why inventory counts drift, and how barcode scanning fixes it

Inventory · Published October 26, 2026 · By the Nastaliq team

The short answer

Inventory counts drift because stock moves or arrives without being recorded at the moment it happens. Scanning barcodes at receiving, transfer and sale, plus small weekly counts, keeps the system and the shelf in step.

You count the shelf on Monday and the system agrees. By Friday it doesn't. Inventory drift is normal, and it almost always comes from the same handful of causes.

Where the numbers go wrong

  • Receiving errors. A delivery is checked against the packing slip, but the slip is wrong, or the count is entered later from memory.
  • Unrecorded movement. Stock moves between rooms, trucks or locations without a transfer being entered.
  • Typing mistakes. One extra zero on a quantity is enough to hide a shortage for weeks.
  • Returns and damage. Items are thrown away or returned to suppliers with no adjustment.
  • Substitutions at the counter. A customer gets a different size than the one rung up.

Why delay makes it worse

The longer the gap between an event and recording it, the more likely it is to be forgotten or entered wrong. Paper notes that get typed in at the end of the day are the biggest source of drift in small warehouses and shops.

What barcode scanning changes

Scanning replaces typing with a camera or scanner. The item is identified by its code, so you can't mistype a name or pick the wrong variant. When scanning happens at the moment of receiving, transferring or selling, the system is updated while the stock is still in your hands.

Habits that keep counts honest

  1. Receive against the purchase order, not against memory.
  2. Record every transfer between locations, even short trips.
  3. Count a little every week. Cycle counts of a few fast-moving items catch problems sooner than one big count a year.
  4. Investigate differences once, then fix the cause, not just the number.
  5. Give staff phone-sized screens so recording takes seconds.

Set a reorder level for important items

When stock is accurate you can trust a reorder alert. A system that tells you six items are below their minimum each morning removes the need to walk the shelves just to find out what to buy.

Start small

You don't need to fix everything at once. Pick your twenty fastest-moving items, scan them in and out for a month and compare against a physical count. Most owners see the difference quickly.

Common questions

Why does my inventory never match the shelf?

Usually because receiving, transfers, returns or damage were recorded late, wrongly or not at all. The longer the gap between the event and the entry, the bigger the drift.

Does barcode scanning really improve stock accuracy?

Yes. Scanning identifies the exact item and records it at the moment it moves, which removes most typing mistakes and forgotten entries.

How often should a warehouse count stock?

Count a small group of fast-moving items every week instead of the whole warehouse once a year. Problems show up sooner and are easier to trace.
