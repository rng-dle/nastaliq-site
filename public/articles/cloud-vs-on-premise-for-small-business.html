Cloud or on-premise software: which fits a small business

Buying guide · Published October 26, 2026 · By the Nastaliq team

Older business software ran on a server in the back office. Newer software runs in the cloud and you reach it through a browser or a phone. For most small businesses the difference comes down to who carries the upkeep.

What on-premise means

You buy or rent a server, install the software, keep it patched and back it up. If it fails, someone on your side fixes it. It can work well for businesses with an IT person and a reason to keep data inside the building.

What cloud means

The vendor hosts the software, applies updates and handles backups. You sign in from anywhere. You pay a subscription instead of buying equipment up front.

Compare the real costs

  • Hardware: servers, networking and replacement after failures.
  • Time: updates, security patches and restoring from backups.
  • Downtime: who gets called when it stops working on a Saturday.
  • Growth: adding a second location or remote staff.

Where cloud fits well

Cloud software suits businesses with several people on phones and tablets, more than one location or no dedicated IT staff. Updates arrive without a visit, and everyone sees the same live numbers.

What to check with a cloud vendor

  1. Can you export all of your data whenever you want?
  2. How often are backups taken and where are they kept?
  3. Are updates and hosting included in the monthly fee?
  4. What is the plan if you lose internet access for a few hours?

When on-premise still makes sense

If your internet connection is unreliable, or a rule or customer contract requires data to stay on site, an on-premise system may be the safer choice. Be honest about whether that is a real requirement or a habit.
