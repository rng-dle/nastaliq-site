How to choose an ERP for a growing business

Buying guide · Published November 11, 2026 · By the Nastaliq team

The short answer

Pick the ERP that completes your own ten everyday tasks live with your data, keeps inventory, purchasing, sales and accounting in one record, runs on an instance that is yours, and has a price that does not rise with headcount.

Most companies choose an ERP the way they chose their first accounting package: a polished demo, a price per user and a gut feeling. A year later the warehouse still runs on a spreadsheet and finance re-types the numbers. This checklist is for owners and operations leads who want to avoid that.

Start with how the business actually runs

Write down what your team does every day. A distributor takes orders, buys from suppliers, receives pallets and invoices customers at agreed prices. A retailer sells, restocks and counts. The system has to fit those moves. If a demo spends ten minutes on features you will never use, that tells you who the product was built for.

Insist on one record, not three

The most common failure is three tools joined by exports: orders in one place, stock in a spreadsheet, the books somewhere else. Ask one question in every demo: when we ship an order, what changes, and where? A good answer is that stock drops, the invoice posts to the books and the customer balance updates, with nobody typing it twice.

Test it where the work happens

Receiving, transfers and counts happen on the warehouse floor. Open the product on a phone and receive a delivery against a purchase order. If you have to pinch and zoom to press a button, your staff will go back to paper.

Ask where your data lives

  • Do you get your own instance, or do you share one system with every other customer?
  • Can you export all of your records, in a normal format, at any time?
  • Who at the vendor can see your data, and why?
  • What does it cost, in time and money, to bring your existing items, customers and balances in?

Look at the pricing model, not only the price

A low price per user gets expensive once sales, warehouse staff and a bookkeeper all need access. Shared logins are the usual workaround, and they destroy your audit trail. Ask whether users are unlimited, whether setup is a one-time fee, and whether hosting and updates are included.

Check who sets it up

Software is half of an ERP project. The other half is configuration, data migration and getting the chart of accounts right. Ask who does that work, whether they understand accounting, and what happens after go-live when something needs to change.

Be honest about what is finished

Ask which features clients use today and which are still being built. A reputable vendor will tell you plainly. Anything labelled "coming soon" should not be part of the decision.

A simple scoring method

  1. List your must-have tasks, no more than ten.
  2. Have each vendor perform each task live, with your own sample data.
  3. Score each one pass or fail.

The system that passes the most real tasks, on an instance you control, with a price you can predict, is usually the right one.

Common questions

What should a growing business look for in an ERP?

Look for one system where a sale, the stock movement and the accounting entry are the same record. Add screens built for each role, warehouse screens that work on a phone, your own instance with full data export, and a price that does not grow with every new user.

How do I compare ERP vendors fairly?

Write down your ten most common tasks, ask each vendor to do them live with your sample data, and score each task pass or fail. Ignore everything else in the demo.

How long does an ERP implementation take?

It depends on how many processes and records you bring across. A setup that starts from an industry preset is usually measured in weeks. Ask each vendor for a dated plan before you sign.
