Shared SaaS, private cloud or on-premise: where should your ERP live?

Buying guide · Published November 11, 2026 · By the Nastaliq team

The short answer

Shared SaaS is the quickest to start but puts your data in a system shared with every other customer. On-premise gives you full control and all of the upkeep. A private cloud instance sits between them: your own system and data, with the vendor carrying the hosting.

An ERP holds the numbers a company least wants to leak or lose: customer prices, supplier costs, margins and the books. Where that system runs decides who looks after it and who can reach it. There are three models, and the differences are practical.

The three models

  • Shared SaaS. Every customer signs in to the same running system. The vendor keeps each company's data apart inside it. It is quick to start and the vendor does all the upkeep, but you get the product as it is, on the vendor's schedule.
  • On-premise. You buy or rent a server, install the software and maintain it. The data never leaves your building, and every patch, backup and failure is yours to handle.
  • Private cloud. You get your own instance, hosted by the vendor. The system and its data are yours alone, and the vendor still carries hosting and updates.

What "kept internal" really means

Owners who say they want operations kept internal usually mean three things: nobody outside the company should see the data, the system should bend to how the company works, and the company should be able to leave with everything it put in. Judge each model against those three, not against the word "cloud".

Compare the real costs

  • Hardware: servers, networking and replacement after failures, for on-premise only.
  • Time: updates, security patches and restoring from backups.
  • Downtime: who gets called when it stops on a Saturday.
  • Growth: what a second location or ten more users adds to the bill.

Where each one fits

Shared SaaS suits companies with standard processes that are happy to adapt to the product. On-premise suits companies with IT staff and a rule or contract that keeps data on site. A private instance suits companies that want their own system, configured to their operations, without running servers.

Questions to ask any vendor

  1. Do we get our own instance, or a place in a shared one?
  2. Can we export all of our data whenever we want?
  3. Who on your side can access our data, and is that access recorded?
  4. How often are backups taken and where are they kept?
  5. What happens to our data if we stop being a customer?

Common questions

What is the difference between shared SaaS and a private cloud ERP?

In shared, or multi-tenant, SaaS every customer uses the same running system and the vendor separates the data inside it. In a private cloud setup each customer has its own instance. You get more separation and more room to configure, while the vendor still handles hosting and updates.

Is an on-premise ERP more secure than a cloud ERP?

Not automatically. Security depends on patching, backups and access control, and all of them take staff time. On-premise gives you physical control and also makes you responsible for every one of those jobs.

What should I ask an ERP vendor about my data?

Ask whether you get your own instance, whether you can export everything at any time, who at the vendor can access your data, how often backups run and where they are kept, and what happens to your data if you leave.
