ERP implementation checklist for growing businesses

Implementation · Published November 12, 2026 · By the Nastaliq team

The short answer

A good ERP implementation starts with a written scope, clean data and named owners, then moves through configuration, testing with real transactions and a planned go-live. Most overruns come from skipped decisions, not from the software.

An ERP project fails quietly, through decisions nobody made. This checklist turns an implementation into a sequence of small, checkable steps.

1. Write down the scope

List the processes the system must cover on day one: purchasing, receiving, sales, invoicing, stock, accounting. Note what waits for later. A scope of one page is enough, as long as everyone has read it.

2. Name an owner and a decision maker

Pick one person on your side who answers questions within a day. Delay in answers is the main cause of delay in delivery.

3. Settle the accounting basics

Agree the chart of accounts, tax settings, currencies, fiscal year and opening balances with your accountant before configuration starts. Changing them afterwards is expensive.

4. Prepare your data

Clean your item list, customers and suppliers, and decide what history comes across. Data migration is its own piece of work, so plan it separately. Our guide to ERP data migration covers what to clean first.

5. List users and roles

Decide who needs access to what. Sales, warehouse, accounting and the owner usually need different screens and different permissions.

6. Configure, then test with real transactions

Run a real purchase from order to supplier bill, and a real sale from quote to payment. Count a shelf and compare it with the system. Testing with made-up examples hides the problems that real work exposes.

7. Train the people who will use it

Short sessions by role work better than one long demo. Give each person a list of the five things they will do most.

8. Plan the go-live

Pick a quiet day, freeze the old system, load the final balances and stock, and keep a list of what to check in the first week. Know who to call if something looks wrong.

9. Review after thirty days

Look at what people work around, what they ask about repeatedly and which reports nobody opens. Small fixes in the first month make the system stick.

Common questions

Who should own an ERP implementation inside the company?

One person with authority to decide, usually the owner or an operations or finance lead. The vendor configures the system, but only you can say how your business actually works.

Should we run the old system and the new one side by side?

Only for a short, defined period. Running both for long doubles the work and invites the two to drift apart. Agree a cut-over date and stick to it.

What is the most common reason implementations slip?

Undecided questions: which accounts to use, who approves what, how stock is counted. Settle them early, in writing.
