How to choose POS and inventory software for a small business

Buying guide · Published October 26, 2026 · By the Nastaliq team

Most small businesses pick point of sale and inventory software the same way: a quick demo, a monthly price, a gut feeling. Six months later the stock numbers are wrong, the books are done by hand and nobody wants to switch again. This checklist helps you avoid that.

Start with how you actually sell

Write down the three or four things your team does every day. A bakery rings sales at a counter and tracks flour and boxes. A distributor takes orders by phone and receives pallets. A shop does both. The software has to fit those moves, not the other way round. If a demo spends ten minutes on features you will never use, that tells you who the product was built for.

Check that sales, stock and books are one system

The most common failure is three tools that don't talk to each other. A sale rings up in one place, stock is edited in a spreadsheet and the accountant gets a monthly export. Ask a simple question during the demo: when I sell one item, what changes, and where? A good answer is that stock drops, the sale posts to the books and the customer record updates, all by itself.

Test it on a phone

Receiving stock, counting shelves and checking a price happen on the floor, not at a desk. Open the product on a phone and try to receive a delivery. If you need to pinch and zoom to press a button, your staff will stop using it.

Ask what happens to your data

  • Can you export all your records, in a normal format, at any time?
  • Who handles backups, and how often?
  • What does it cost, in time and money, to bring your existing customers and products in?

Look at the pricing model, not only the price

A low price per user gets expensive when you add cashiers, warehouse staff and a bookkeeper. Ask whether users are unlimited, whether setup is a one-time fee and whether hosting and updates are included. A flat monthly subscription is easier to plan around than a bill that changes whenever you hire.

Check language and location

If you serve customers in English and French, check that invoices, receipts and staff screens can switch language, not just the website. Check tax handling for your province as well.

Be honest about what is finished

Ask the vendor which features are available today and which are still being built. Reputable vendors will tell you plainly. Anything labelled "coming soon" should not be part of your buying decision.

A simple scoring method

  1. List your must-have tasks, no more than ten.
  2. Have each vendor show you each task live, with your own sample data.
  3. Score each one pass or fail. Ignore everything else.

The system that passes the most real tasks, with a pricing model you can predict, is usually the right one.
