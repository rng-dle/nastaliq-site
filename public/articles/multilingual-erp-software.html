Multilingual ERP software: what to check before you buy

Buying guide · Published November 12, 2026 · By the Nastaliq team

The short answer

Good multilingual ERP software lets each user pick a language and prints invoices and receipts in the language your customer reads. Test it with your own documents before you buy.

If your team, your suppliers or your customers work in more than one language, the software has to keep up. A system that only works well in one language creates friction on every side of the counter. This is what to check.

Where language shows up

  • Customer documents: invoices, quotes, receipts and statements.
  • Staff screens: menus, forms, buttons and error messages.
  • Reports and emails: the digest the owner reads each morning and the notices customers receive.
  • Product data: item names and descriptions that customers see.

Translation is not the same as fluency

A screen can be technically translated and still read badly. Business vocabulary for receipts, taxes and payment terms varies between regions, and staff notice when a screen sounds foreign. Ask the vendor who maintains the translations and how a wrong term gets fixed.

Questions to ask a vendor

  1. Can each user choose their own language, or is it one setting for the whole company?
  2. Can one customer receive documents in one language while another receives a different one?
  3. Are reports, emails and error messages translated, or only the main screens?
  4. Does the system handle languages written right to left?
  5. Can we correct a term ourselves when it does not fit our trade?

Check your own obligations

Language requirements for commercial documents differ by province, state and business type. Speak to your accountant or adviser about what applies to you, and make sure your software can meet it.

The practical benefit

A multilingual system lets you hire from a wider pool, serve more customers and hand documents to either side without retyping. For a growing business that sells across borders, that matters more each year.

What to do next

Before you buy, ask for a demo in each language you need, using your own product names and one of your real invoices. If the system handles each one cleanly, you have one less thing to worry about later. Nastaliq runs in the language your team works in, with each user choosing their own.

Common questions

Do all users have to work in the same language?

They should not. A good system lets each user choose their own language, so a warehouse team and an accounting team can work in different languages on the same data.

Can one customer get documents in a different language from another?

It should. Ask the vendor to show an invoice for one customer in one language and an invoice for another customer in a different language from the same system.

Are translations complete?

Not always. Ask to see your own screens, reports and emails in the language you need, and ask who fixes a term that reads wrong.
